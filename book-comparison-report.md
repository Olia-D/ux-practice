# Book Comparison: UX Practice App vs. "Just Enough Research" (2nd ed) by Erika Hall

## Overview

The UX Practice app is based on Trevor Calabro's "UX Research in the Wild" newsletter (26 articles across 6 modules). "Just Enough Research" is a foundational book covering the full UX research lifecycle. This report compares the two for conflicts, gaps, and enrichment opportunities.

**Bottom line: No conflicts found. The two sources are highly compatible.** The newsletter goes deeper on practical design patterns and career topics; the book goes deeper on research methods, organizational dynamics, and statistical foundations. The app should receive a few small enrichments — not wholesale additions of new book topics, which would break its scope.

### Book topics NOT in the app (by design — out of newsletter scope)

These are substantial book topics the app doesn't cover. They're not gaps to fill; they're different scope.

- **Surveys** (Ch. 9) — sampling math, confidence levels, Likert scales, NPS critique
- **Personas** (Ch. 8) — creation, goals, behaviors, demographics, scenarios
- **Mental Models** (Ch. 8) — diagramming user mental models, conceptual models
- **Contextual Inquiry** (Ch. 5) — field observation, ethnographic methods
- **Organizational Research** (Ch. 4) — full stakeholder interview methodology
- **Recruiting** (Ch. 3 & 7) — screeners, participant databases, compensation
- **Ethics in Research** (Ch. 2) — informed consent, privacy, deception
- **Literature Review** (Ch. 3) — desk research, existing studies
- **Task Analysis as output** (Ch. 8) — breaking tasks into cognitive + physical steps
- **Affinity Diagrams** (Ch. 8) — 6-step synthesis process

**Recommendation:** Don't add these. The app is scoped to the newsletter. A future "Module 7: Research Methods Deep Dive" could reference the book, but that's a separate decision.

## Specific Enrichments to Apply

### 1. mod1-usability-vs-concept — Add Nielsen's 5 components
**Learn card 1**, after the compare visual. Add one line:
> Usability testing evaluates 5 specific qualities (Nielsen): learnability, efficiency, memorability, error recovery, and satisfaction.

This gives learners the vocabulary they'll encounter in practice.

### 2. mod2-cognitive-biases — Add "validate" language warning
**Learn card 2** (How we fool ourselves), add to confirmation bias section:
> **Watch your language:** Erika Hall warns against saying "validate the design" — it frames research as proving you're right, not as learning. Say "evaluate" or "test." Aim to be proven wrong.

### 3. mod1-research-rigor — Add "cheap tests first" principle
**Learn card 2** (Can we reverse it?), add:
> **Sequence by cost:** Test with paper prototypes before interactive ones. Test with colleagues before recruiting users. Test competitors before building your own. Each cheaper round eliminates issues the expensive round doesn't need to find.

---

## Conclusion

The app and the book are complementary, not competing. The book provides foundational research methodology; the app distills practical, newsletter-length insights for working designers. Three small enrichments from the book would strengthen the app without changing its character. No content needs to be deleted or corrected.
