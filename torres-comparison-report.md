# Torres Comparison Report
## UX Practice App vs. Continuous Discovery Habits (Teresa Torres)

**Date:** 2026-11-15
**Scope:** All 26 articles compared against Torres's book

---

## Summary

No conflicts found between the app content and Torres's framework. Torres's continuous discovery approach complements the app's UX research focus — she operates at the product strategy layer while the app focuses on tactical UX research execution. Five articles were enriched with Torres insights where the overlap was strongest.

---

## Enrichments Made

### 1. Cognitive Biases in UX Research (mod2-cognitive-biases)
**Added:** Torres's rule about always testing multiple ideas in parallel. If you only evaluate one solution, confirmation bias guarantees you'll find reasons to love it. Directly supports the existing confirmation bias section with a practical countermeasure.

### 2. A/B Testing and the Two-Party Trap (mod3-ab-testing)
**Added:** Torres's "test assumptions, not ideas" as an alternative to traditional A/B testing. A 10-person simulation test on one assumption takes a day vs. weeks for a full A/B test. Complements the article's critique of weak A/B tests.

### 3. UX Research Debriefs Reframed (mod5-debrief)
**Added:** Torres's interview snapshot method — a one-page synthesis after each interview instead of batch synthesis at the end. Creates a searchable index over time. Directly complements the synthesis vs. strategic session framework.

### 4. Users Don't Care About Your Figma File (mod5-figma)
**Added:** Torres/Perri's "build trap" concept — teams obsess over shipping features instead of measuring outcomes. Reinforces the article's core message that internal quality metrics are not UX quality metrics.

### 5. UX Research as a Service (mod2-ux-as-service)
**Added:** Torres's product trio concept — designer, PM, and engineer doing discovery together, not handing off between roles. Each role spots different signals from the same interview. Strengthens the "show up during shaping" message.

## Topics Reviewed but Not Enriched

These articles were reviewed against Torres and found to be either complete as-is or addressing topics Torres doesn't cover directly:

- **Usability vs. Concept Tests** — Torres discusses assumption testing but her methods differ from the usability/concept distinction. Already enriched with Nielsen's 5 usability qualities from Hall.
- **Behavioral Evidence** — Torres emphasizes behavior over self-report in interviews but the article already covers this thoroughly.
- **Research Rigor** — Already enriched with Hall's "cheap tests first" principle, which overlaps with Torres's "start small, iterate bigger."
- **Cognitive Walkthrough** — Expert evaluation method; Torres focuses on customer-facing methods.
- **Fake AI Users** — Torres would agree (real users are essential) but doesn't address AI-generated research specifically.
- **A/B Tests Mislead** — Torres's false positive/negative discussion is more about small-sample assumption tests, not A/B confounds.
- **Mixed Methods** — Torres mentions triangulation but doesn't detail mixed-method designs.
- **Top 10 Misunderstandings** — NNG/Baymard reference material; no Torres overlap.
- **Design Principles (Module 4)** — Icons, affordances, animations, input fields, status design — all tactical UI patterns outside Torres's scope.
- **ROI of UX** — Torres focuses on outcomes but doesn't cover ROI calculation frameworks.
- **Rethinking Delight** — Torres doesn't address delight specifically.
- **Dashboard Design** — Data visualization; outside Torres's scope.
- **Career & Influence (Module 6)** — IC career, thankless UX, institutional knowledge, predictions — all outside Torres's scope.

## Key Takeaway

Torres's book and the app content are complementary, not competing. Torres provides the strategic discovery framework (opportunity solution trees, continuous interviewing, assumption testing). The app provides the tactical UX research execution (how to run studies, avoid biases, analyze data, prove impact). A practitioner benefits from both layers.
